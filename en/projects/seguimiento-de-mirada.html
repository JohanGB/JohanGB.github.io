01Problem

It estimates where you are looking on the screen with a webcam, warns you after more than 3 seconds looking away, and on exit generates a heatmap of your fixations. Everything runs locally on the CPU: no frame is written to disk or leaves the computer.

02Data

  • A ~90-second click-free calibration: follow a circle with your head still, then moving it slowly, then look at 9 + 5 fixed points.
  • First real data: one person, three sessions.

03Method

  • Face and iris landmarks with MediaPipe; head pose with solvePnP; the camera–iris ray is intersected with the eyeball to get a 3D gaze vector.
  • Ridge with an RBF-kernel corrector on the residual, and a One Euro filter for smoothing.
  • Why Ridge, not SVR: an RBF-SVR regresses to the mean outside the training data; with the real gaze at 1.25 screen widths, Ridge flagged "off-screen" 100 % of the time, SVR 0 %.

04Validation

  • Fixed points are never used for training: they measure the real error. Accuracy (bias) and precision (jitter) are reported separately.
  • Median error in simulation (1920×1080): calibration pose, 49 px classic vs. 45 px 3D geometry; 20° head yaw, 532 px vs. 87 px; combined movement, 593 px vs. 167 px.
  • Personal calibration cut moved-head error from 100–245 px to 17–37 px. CPU: about 25 % of one core at 15 FPS.
  • 87 automated tests (85 pass in CI; 2 are skipped without the downloaded MediaPipe model); the UI is tested with real threads and headless Qt.

05Limitations

  • Not yet validated with multiple real users; first real data showed excellent horizontal tracking (R² 0.97–0.98) but weak vertical tracking.
  • 640×480 resolution limits precision to screen zones, not words.
  • Extreme poses roughly double the error. Single monitor.
